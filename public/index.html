Banyan 2026 Growth Challenge

$3M to change the trajectory of your business

Every Banyan operating company can pitch one idea: a new product, market, channel or capability that would unlock growth you cannot reach on your current plan. Banyan will fund the strongest proposals from a $3M pool, and the money sits outside your OpEx budget and bonus calculations so it does not compete with the business you already run. Tell us what you would build, how you would execute it, what it costs, which market it opens, and what it returns within 12 months.

What to include in 300 words

The idea

What are you proposing to build, launch, or change? Say it plainly, in one or two sentences a CEO in another vertical would understand.

Execution plan

Who does the work, what happens in the first 90 days, and what has to be true for it to succeed. Name the biggest risk.

Specific capital ask

A number, in your currency, with a rough breakdown of where it goes. Awarded funding is excluded from your OpEx budget and bonus calculations.

New or incremental market unlock

Which customers, segment, geography or product line does this open up that you cannot reach today? What signal tells you they want it?

ROI potential within 12 months

Show the math: incremental ARR, margin, or cost avoided against the ask. A credible, specific estimate with visible assumptions beats a large round number.
Process

How proposals are judged

Round 0
Submit
One 300-word proposal per operating company through this portal. Submissions close Friday, October 2 at 11:59 PM ET.
Round 1
Operating Group
Your Group President, Portfolio Leader and Chief of Staff each rate every proposal in the group 1 to 5. The top 2 per Operating Group advance.
Round 2
Semifinalists
Roughly 20 proposals scored independently across five dimensions: vision for value creation, market signals, 12-month ROI, asymmetric upside, and execution risk. About 10 advance.
Round 3
Finalists
Banyan leadership may request additional detail from CEOs, then allocates the $3M across the strongest proposals based on the ask, size of opportunity and business performance.
Write it yourself

We are reading for judgment, not polish. Three hundred words is enough to show you understand the customer, the economics and the risk. Proposals that read like they were generated rather than thought through will not do well with the reviewers, many of whom know your business. Use AI to sharpen your thinking if you like, but make sure every claim is one you would defend in the room.
